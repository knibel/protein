## 1. Reference patterns (WHO TRS 935 2007 and FAO FNP 92 2013), mg per g protein

### Takeaway
For a general adult/family app use the FAO 2013 "older child, adolescent, adult" pattern (= 3-10 y pattern): His 16, Ile 30, Leu 61, Lys 48, SAA 23, AAA 41, Thr 25, Trp 6.6, Val 40. The strict adult (>18 y) maintenance pattern from TRS 935 (His 15, Ile 30, Leu 59, Lys 45, SAA 22, AAA 38, Thr 23, Trp 6.0, Val 39) is slightly lower and would flatter scores; FAO deliberately recommends the 3-10 y pattern for everyone over 3 y. If the app is meant for families with toddlers, offer the 0.5-3 y pattern as a second mode.

### Inferences
- Why the 3-10 y/FAO 2013 pattern for an adult/family app: it is the official FAO recommendation for everyone over 3 y, is about 3-8% higher than the adult maintenance pattern (conservative), and adult requirement estimates themselves have residual uncertainty (TRS 935 8.3: "some uncertainty remains"). The adult values are within a few mg/g of it, so rankings will barely change.
- The ranking of complements is not very sensitive to the pattern: Lys (48 vs 57) and SAA (23 vs 27) are the discriminating amino acids in cereal-legume pairings.

### Gaps
- Per-amino-acid breakdown of AAA into Phe/Tyr and SAA into Met/Cys in the FAO 2013 pattern was not retrieved (TRS 935 Table 23 gives Met 16/Cys 6 only for the adult requirement).

## 2. Definitions: AAS, PDCAAS, DIAAS and digestibility values

### Takeaway
AAS = lowest ratio of (mg of each IAA per g food protein)/(mg per g reference protein). PDCAAS = AAS x true faecal crude-protein digestibility, truncated at 100%. DIAAS = 100 x lowest ratio of digestible IAA (IAA content x true ileal digestibility of that amino acid)/reference, not truncated for single foods. FAO 2013 prefers DIAAS, but for an app without per-amino-acid ileal digestibility data, a PDCAAS-style single digestibility factor is the pragmatic fallback (FAO itself says so for the interim).

### Cited Findings
- DIAAS definition [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, FAO FNP 92 p. 23]: "DIAAS % = 100 x lowest value [(mg of digestible dietary indispensable amino acid in 1 g of the dietary protein)/(mg of the same dietary indispensable amino acid in 1 g of the reference protein)]". The main difference to PDCAAS is true ileal digestibility for each IAA instead of a single faecal crude-protein digestibility. [FAO FNP 92](https://www.fao.org/3/i3124e/i3124e.pdf)
- FAO 2013 on truncation [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]: DIAAS values above 100 should not be truncated for individual foods/ingredients (so that complementing ability is visible), but "The DIAAS for a mixed diet itself should be truncated" (at 100). "A DIAAS over 100 indicates potential to complement protein of lower quality provided that a suitable total N intake is maintained." Also: DIAAS may be used to adjust protein intake only when <=100.
- FAO interim advice [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, p. 5 of recommendations]: until an agreed true-ileal dataset for human foods exists, protein should still be assessed using DIAAS but "digestible individual dietary amino acid values should be calculated using faecal crude protein digestibility values applied to dietary amino acid contents".
- PDCAAS (TRS 935 6.1, FAO/WHO 1991): AAS (first-limiting amino acid vs reference) x true protein digestibility; TRS 935 discusses truncation and notes the 1991 report truncated PDCAAS but not the AAS [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, TRS 935 section 6.2]. TRS 935 also states that for a mixture, "amino acid score is calculated for the mixture from its overall amino acid profile without identifying the score of component proteins" and "should be calculated from the weighted average digestible amino acid content", in contrast to the 1991 report [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, FAO FNP 92 text, summarizing 2007 report].
- FAO quality categories [VERIFIED-SECONDARY, [Herreman et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7590266/)]: DIAAS <75 no quality claim; 75-99 high; >=100 excellent.
- Digestibility examples used by FAO 2013, true ileal digestibility (CVB feed tables, predicted human values from pig data) [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, FAO Tables 1 and 2, p. 24-25]:
  - Whole milk powder: Lys 0.95, SAA 0.94, Thr 0.90, Trp 0.90.
  - Wheat: Lys 0.82, SAA 0.895, Thr 0.86, Trp 0.91.
  - Pea: Lys 0.79, SAA 0.69, Thr 0.73, Trp 0.66.
  - Composition per g protein used there: wheat Lys 28, SAA 38, Thr 29, Trp 12 (mg/g); pea Lys 71, SAA 25, Thr 37, Trp 9; milk powder Lys 78, SAA 35, Thr 44, Trp 13. Protein: wheat 11, pea 21, milk powder 28 g/100 g.
  - FAO Table 6 example DIAAS (child 0.5-3 y pattern): wheat 40, pea 64, whole milk powder 122.
- Mean DIAAS by food, 0.5-3 y pattern, growing-pig SID [VERIFIED-PRIMARY via PMC XML of [Herreman et al. 2020 Food Sci Nutr 8:5379, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7590266/)] (limiting AA in brackets): casein 117, pork 117, egg 101, potato 100, soy 91 (SAA), whey 85 (His), canola 72 (Lys), lupin 68 (SAA), rapeseed 67 (Lys), pea 70 (SAA), oat 57 (Lys), fava bean 55 (SAA), hemp 54 (Lys), wheat 48 (Lys), rice 47 (Lys), corn 36 (Lys), gelatin 2 (Trp). Per-amino-acid DIAA scores (e.g. wheat Lys 48, SAA 127; pea Lys 110, SAA 70; soy Lys 96, SAA 91; egg Lys 133, SAA 123) are in that table; large between-dataset SD (e.g. egg Lys +-58).
- Typical true faecal protein digestibility (PDCAAS inputs) [UNVERIFIED from primary]: a secondary blog gives whole egg 97%, milk 95%, isolated soy 95% ([source](https://blog.yeswelab.fr/en/agrifood/pdcaas-analysis/)). Commonly cited FAO/WHO 1991 values from memory (not verified this session, treat as approximate): egg 97, milk/casein 95, soy protein isolate 95, beef 94, wheat (whole) ~86-91, rice ~88-90, oats ~86, pea ~88, kidney beans/lentils/chickpea ~72-85, peanut ~94, maize ~85. A search returned rice 90% digestibility / PDCAAS ~0.86 from one secondary source [UNVERIFIED].
- Ileal vs faecal: FAO 2013 concluded ileal digestibility is preferable because faecal digestibility is distorted by hindgut microbial metabolism; humans' true ileal data are scarce, the pig is the preferred model [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, FAO recommendations pp. 3-5].

### Inferences
- For the app: store per-food (a) EAA mg/g protein (9 values), (b) protein g/100 g, (c) one digestibility coefficient (faecal true digestibility as PDCAAS fallback, or per-amino-acid ileal digestibility if a dataset is obtained). Use DIAAS-style non-truncated scores for single foods; truncate only the mixture score.
- Plant digestibility values vary a lot by processing, cultivar and anti-nutritional factors (Herreman 2020), so display rankings as approximate.

### Gaps
- No primary table of FAO/WHO 1991 faecal digestibility values was retrieved; the numeric digestibility list above is from memory and must be checked (FAO FNP 51, 1991, Table 5/11, or USDA SR data supplements) before use.
- Per-food ileal digestibility datasets for human foods (e.g. the pig dataset in Herreman Table S1) were not downloaded.

## 3. Computing the score of a mixture and the optimal mixing ratio

### Takeaway
Pool amino acids, not scores: mix on a protein-weight basis, compute the weighted mean of each (digestible) IAA per g protein, divide by the reference, take the minimum. The score of a two-food blend as a function of protein fraction w is the lower envelope of nine straight lines, so it is concave and piecewise linear; its maximum lies at an endpoint or at an intersection of two lines, and can be found exactly by checking at most 36 crossing points.

### Cited Findings
- Authority for the method: TRS 935 and FAO 2013 both calculate the mixture score from the total (digestible) IAA per g total protein in the mixture, not from averaging component scores [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]. FAO Table 2 worked example (400 g wheat + 100 g pea + 35 g whole milk powder, adult pattern): total protein 75 g; digestible Lys 2914 mg, SAA 2172, Thr 2052, Trp 720; per g protein: Lys 38.9, SAA 29.0, Thr 27.4, Trp 9.6; ratios vs adult pattern (48/23/25/6.6): 0.82 / 1.26 / 1.10 / 1.45; DIAAS 82 (Lys). Child 0.5-3 y pattern gives 68, infant 56. [FAO FNP 92](https://www.fao.org/3/i3124e/i3124e.pdf)
- TRS 935 Table 6 PDCAAS example (wheat 400 g + chickpea 100 g + milk powder 35 g): weighted digestibility 0.85 (sum of digestible protein/total protein), AAS ratios Lys 0.99 (adult pattern), PDCAAS 0.84 [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, TRS 935 Table 6]. Note the ratio uses mg per g of digestible protein in the mixture and then multiplies by weighted average digestibility (equivalent to mixing on digestible protein only for a single-digestibility PDCAAS).
- TRS 935 conclusion [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, section 8/6]: on the new scoring pattern "mixtures of cereal proteins with relatively modest amounts of legumes or oil seeds, or animal proteins are unlikely to be limited through their amino acid content."
- Herreman et al. 2020 [VERIFIED-PRIMARY via PMC XML]: for two proteins with a digestible-IAA content c_{y,1} and c_{y,2} for amino acid y and protein fraction R1 (R2 = 1 - R1): DIAA_y = c_{y,1} R1 + c_{y,2} (1 - R1); DIAAS(P1+...+Pn) = 100 x lowest DIAA of the mixture; optimum found with Excel Solver, max DIAAS limited to <=100. Results on 0.5-3 y pattern (Table 3, ratios are protein-weight fractions): pea/wheat 60/40 gives 85; soy/wheat 90/10 gives 90; soy/oat 90/10 gives 92; soy/canola 85/15 gives 92; fava/corn 75/25 gives 64; fava/rapeseed 55/45 gives 82; canola/pea 35/65 gives 84; corn/soy 15/85 gives 88; oat/lupin 7/93 gives 76; wheat/potato 30/70, corn/potato 25/75 and lupin/potato 30/70 give 100. The paper notes not all legume-cereal pairs help: if Lys is already second or third limiting in the legume (e.g. lupin Lys 75), the gain is minimal. [Herreman et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7590266/)

### Algorithm (for implementation)
Definitions: for food f, e_{f,i} = digestible IAA i in mg per g protein (= content_i x digestibility_i, or content_i x single digestibility d_f for the PDCAAS fallback); ref_i = reference pattern (Section 1); r_{f,i} = e_{f,i}/ref_i.
1. Single food, non-truncated score: S_f = 100 x min_i r_{f,i}; limiting amino acid = argmin.
2. Mixture of foods with protein masses m_f (g protein): e_i = sum_f m_f e_{f,i} / sum_f m_f. S = min(100, 100 x min_i e_i/ref_i). (With one common digestibility factor, mixing digestible protein masses is equivalent to the TRS 935 weighted digestibility; with per-amino-acid digestibilities use digestible amounts as in FAO Table 2.)
3. Two foods A (focus food) and B (candidate), protein fraction w of A: r_i(w) = w r_{A,i} + (1-w) r_{B,i} (linear in w). S(w) = 100 x min_i r_i(w), concave piecewise linear.
4. Optimum: candidate w values = {0, 1} plus, for each pair (i, j) with r_i(w) = r_j(w): w_ij = (r_{B,j} - r_{B,i}) / ((r_{A,i} - r_{B,i}) - (r_{A,j} - r_{B,j})) whenever the denominator is nonzero and 0 <= w_ij <= 1. Evaluate S at each candidate; w* = argmax. A simplification valid for the typical cereal-legume case: only the pair (limiting AA of A, limiting AA of B) matters; with A lysine-limited and B SAA-limited, set w r_{A,Lys} + (1-w) r_{B,Lys} = w r_{A,SAA} + (1-w) r_{B,SAA}, so w* = (r_{B,SAA} - r_{B,Lys}) / ((r_{A,Lys} - r_{B,Lys}) - (r_{A,SAA} - r_{B,SAA})), provided no third amino acid becomes limiting (check by evaluating all nine).
5. Convert the protein fraction to food amounts: grams of food = w x P / p_A (p = protein g per g food), likewise for B; ratio of food masses = (w/p_A):((1-w)/p_B). Show a realistic range (e.g. weights giving S >= 0.95 x S_max) rather than a single sharp ratio, since the optimum is a corner of a concave function and shallow.
6. Ranking of candidates for a chosen food A (suggested): primary key = best achievable S*(A,B) = max_w S(w) (capped at 100); secondary key = gain over A alone (S* - S_A) and the protein fraction needed from A at w*; flag candidates whose w* is below ~10% or above ~90% as "barely a combination". Alternative single-ratio metric: S at equal protein shares (w = 0.5) or at a fixed realistic portion ratio. Optionally penalise candidates with low protein density (g protein per 100 kcal or per 100 g) because a complement providing little protein cannot shift the profile at realistic portions: the achievable w is limited by portion sizes.
7. Also report what each candidate fixes: for A's limiting amino acid(s) show r_{B,i}; list "complements" as foods with r_{B,lim(A)} >= 1 (a rule of thumb that also works without optimisation, and consistent with FAO's statement that a DIAAS >100 food complements lower-quality proteins).

### Inferences
- The "limiting amino acid" of A may change in the blend, so always evaluate all nine; including AAA/SAA as sums of Phe+Tyr and Met+Cys as in the FAO pattern.
- Tryptophan can be unmeasured in some datasets (Herreman: hemp dataset lacked Trp); handle missing values by skipping with a warning.
- Synergy is only real if both foods are consumed within the nutritionally relevant time frame (see Section 4) and total protein supply is adequate.

### Gaps
- Closed-form w* above is derived by me from the linear-mixing formula, not quoted from a source; numerically check it in the JS tests against Herreman Table 3 (e.g. pea/wheat 60/40 gives 85 on mean data).
- Sensitivity of the ranking to missing digestibility data and to cooked/processed lysine (Maillard "reactive lysine", FAO recommends reactive lysine) not quantified.

## 4. Same meal versus across the day

### Takeaway
Current professional consensus: complementary proteins do not need to be eaten at the same meal, spread over the day is sufficient, provided energy and total protein are adequate and varied plant foods are eaten. The physiological basis (amino acid pools, 24-h balance) is plausible but direct human evidence is limited; the best recent direct test (Arentson-Lantz 2024) found no difference in 24-h muscle protein synthesis, but it is small. Thus the app should present complementarity as a useful but not mandatory meal-level tool.

### Cited Findings
- Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, Position on Vegetarian Diets (Melina, Craig, Levin; J Acad Nutr Diet 2016;116:1970-1980, doi 10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025; [PubMed 27886704](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27886704/)): full text is paywalled and could not be read this session [UNVERIFIED wording]. From memory the paper states that plant proteins eaten over the course of a day can supply adequate essential amino acids if energy needs are met and varied plant foods are consumed, and that complementary proteins need not be eaten at the same meal. Needs verification against the full text before quoting. The abstract confirms only that well-planned vegetarian/vegan diets are adequate.
- DGE (Deutsche Gesellschaft für Ernährung) position on vegan diet, Ernährungs Umschau 4/2016 [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, text read]: recommends to eat legumes, nuts, whole grains, oilseeds and potatoes "über den Tag verteilt und gezielt kombiniert" (spread over the day and purposefully combined, e.g. cereal + legumes, soy products and/or oilseeds); states that through the targeted combination of various plant protein sources the protein quality of the daily protein intake and thus the intake of all indispensable amino acids can be increased (cites refs 88, 89); cites Young and Pellett that a purely plant-based diet with combination of protein sources can allow normal growth of children. [DGE position PDF](https://www.dge.de/fileadmin/dok/wissenschaft/positionen/EU04_2016_M220-M230_korr.pdf). The DGE FAQ rationale: legumes low in SAA, wheat low in lysine, combination raises protein quality via "Ergänzungswirkung". Note: DGE says its 2016 and 2020 vegan positions are no longer valid after a 2024 reassessment ([DGE FAQ](https://www.dge.de/gesunde-ernaehrung/faq/faqs-vegane-ernaehrung/)); I did not read the 2024 reassessment, so its protein wording is [UNVERIFIED]. Importantly the DGE wording is "over the day", not "in the same meal".
- Young VR, Pellett PL. Plant proteins in relation to human protein and amino acid nutrition. Am J Clin Nutr 1994;59(5 Suppl):1203S-1212S. Abstract-level claims [VERIFIED-SECONDARY via PubMed search snippet]: plant proteins supply ~65% of per-capita protein worldwide; mixtures of plant proteins can serve as a complete and well-balanced source of amino acids. The abstract does not address meal timing; the "no need for same meal" claim in secondary sources is not confirmed from the paper text [UNVERIFIED]. [PubMed 8172124](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8172124/)
- Contrary/qualifying note: a clinical trial registry entry (NCT07151625) states that the idea that complementation within a meal is unnecessary if amino acids are eaten within 24 h "has never been tested" and attributes to the Young/Pellett lab the view that there is no storage pool of amino acids [UNVERIFIED, registry description via search snippet].
- Direct human test: Arentson-Lantz EJ et al., J Nutr 2024;154(12):3626-3638, doi 10.1016/j.tjnut.2024.10.010. 9 healthy middle-aged women, randomised crossover, 23 g protein per meal from beef (complete), beans + whole wheat bread together (complementary), or single incomplete proteins at separate meals (complete over 24 h): no difference in muscle protein synthesis after breakfast (P = 0.90) or over 24 h (P = 0.38); all differ from a 5 g protein control only for complete/complementary breakfast. [VERIFIED-SECONDARY via [UTMB record](https://researchexperts.utmb.edu/en/publications/meals-containing-equivalent-total-protein-from-foods-providing-co/)]. Small n, a single outcome (MPS), not nitrogen balance.
- FAO 2013 explicit framing [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]: DIAAS is for "mixed diets" as well as individual foods, and values for mixed diets are meant at the diet level; no statement forcing same-meal pairing found in the text.

### Inferences
- The app should compute scores at "meal/combination" level for the user's chosen pairing (conservative) and state that the same benefit is expected over a day (DGE/AND-type consensus).
- Prefer wording like "ergänzt sich gut" over "muss zusammen gegessen werden".

### Gaps
- AND 2016 exact wording not obtained (paywall); Young & Pellett full text not accessible; DGE 2024 reassessment not read; no other position papers (e.g. EFSA, WHO) searched.

## 5. Classic pairing evidence and caveats

### Takeaway
Cereals are lysine-limited and legumes SAA-limited (and soy/pea nearly adequate in Lys); the numeric data confirm this and show that cereal + legume blends at roughly 60:40 to 90:10 protein shares can reach DIAAS 85-92 vs 47-48 for the cereal alone. Caveats are digestibility, the cereal-to-legume protein ratio, Lys already limiting in some legumes, and processing losses of lysine.

### Cited Findings
- Cereals low in Lys, high in SAA; legumes the reverse [VERIFIED-PRIMARY data]: FAO Table 2 per g protein, wheat Lys 28, SAA 38 vs pea Lys 71, SAA 25 (the adult pattern is Lys 48, SAA 23); DIAA scores in Herreman Table 2: wheat Lys 48 / SAA 127; rice Lys 47 / SAA 104; corn Lys 36 / SAA 126; oat Lys 57 / SAA 151; pea Lys 110 / SAA 70; fava Lys 95 / SAA 55; lupin Lys 75 / SAA 68; soy Lys 96 / SAA 91 (all % of 0.5-3 y pattern). [Herreman 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7590266/)
- Herreman: "Cereal-based proteins, scoring low in Lys but high in Met + Cys, can to some extent complement leguminous proteins, scoring high in Lys but low in Met + Cys"; potato, soy and pea proteins can complement a broad range of plant proteins; "Not all leguminous/cereal protein combinations will lead to higher scores".
- Pairing examples with mix ratios: see Section 3 (pea/wheat 60/40 gives 85; soy/wheat 90/10 gives 90; canola/pea 35/65 gives 84; fava/corn 75/25 only 64 because corn Lys 36 and fava SAA 55 are both very low).
- TRS 935: with the (higher) 2007 scoring pattern, cereals + modest legume/oilseed/animal protein are unlikely to be limited [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]; digestibility, not amino acid score, is the main concern with some plant diets (TRS 935, section 6) [VERIFIED-PRIMARY].
- Examples DIAAS for mixes in FAO Table 2: wheat + pea + milk powder gives 82 (adult pattern), Lys-limited, illustrating that a Lys-poor cereal dominates mass-wise.
- Caveat on rapid reliability: human biological value differences are hard to demonstrate; TRS 935 notes inter-individual variability of BV up to 50% and poor between-study reproducibility, so score differences of a few points are not meaningful [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, TRS 935 section 6].

### Inferences
- Because a portion of cereal brings much more protein mass than a small legume side, w in the optimisation is bounded by realistic portions; the app should show both the theoretical optimum and the score at realistic portion sizes.

### Gaps
- Classic experimental evidence (e.g. Bressani/INCAP corn-bean studies, Hegsted, nitrogen balance on rice-bean mixes) was not retrieved; cited only through the reports above.
- Effect of cooking/processing on available (reactive) lysine and legume anti-nutritional factors is flagged by FAO 2013 but not quantified here.
